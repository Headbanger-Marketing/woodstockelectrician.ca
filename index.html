Before work begins

Think about the route between buildings first.

A property with a detached garage or workshop may have a long feeder from the house carrying lights, receptacles and tools. Wherever a cable has to go, in that home or another, its path shapes the job, not just the load it serves.

Sketch the path from the house to each outbuilding in words, noting what lies between, such as a driveway, a patio or a garden bed. Ask who owns any shared fence line before the contractor plans a route.

Does a longer run to a garage change what the work involves?

It can. A longer route between buildings may affect the cable, the protection at each end and the trenching or conduit work. Ask the contractor to explain how the route, not just the load, shaped the proposal.

What records help when a feeder to an outbuilding already exists?

Gather any ESA paperwork, drawings or invoices that show how the outbuilding was supplied. Photograph the visible equipment at each end. Where nothing is on file, say so, because the contractor then has more to establish.

How do I compare quotes when the cable route differs?

Put the quotes side by side and check that each describes the same start point, end point and route. A quote that assumes a different path is not a like-for-like comparison, so ask each contractor to state it.

Who decides whether the route can cross a neighbour's land?

The route must stay on land you can authorize, and any crossing of someone else's property needs their agreement and possibly the municipality's. Raise this early so the contractor does not price a path that cannot be used.

If I submit this form, who comes out to the garage and house?

Only a provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you engage comes out; it confirms its name and licence, and the job with cost and dates, separately.
