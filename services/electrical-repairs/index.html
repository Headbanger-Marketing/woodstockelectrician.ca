A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Woodstock property

Write down which outdoor receptacle, fixture or outbuilding circuit stopped, when you noticed, and what the weather and use were at the time. Do not touch anything that looks scorched, wet or damaged.

Ask the contractor whether the fault lies in the equipment, the cable run to the building or the protection at the house end, and what diagnostic scope the visit covers before repair is approved.

What should the assessment establish?

When outdoor or damp-location equipment at a Woodstock property stops working, the report gives what was found and how far along the route from the house the problem lies. Power coming back does not close the matter: equipment that works again is still entered as suited to its exposure, or as unsuited. The scope keeps diagnosis apart from any repair and names each part to be replaced. If the cause lies in a buried or concealed section, the report explains what happens next. Anything that could not be inspected is listed in the report.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Why not keep switching the outdoor breaker back on after it trips?

A device that trips again straight away is signalling something, so stop using that circuit and leave the equipment alone. Describe how often it happened and what was running for the contractor.

Is a dead outlet in the workshop a house problem or a garage problem?

That is for the contractor to establish. Note whether other lights or receptacles in the same building also stopped, and whether anything in the house went off at the same time.

Useful project details

  • Observed symptom and when it began
  • Rooms or devices affected
  • Equipment operating at the time
  • Recent alterations and previous repairs
  • Owner or manager who can authorize access and repairs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
