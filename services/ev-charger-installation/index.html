EV charger installation in Woodstock.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Woodstock property

Mark where the vehicle will park, how far that is from the panel and whether the route passes through a garage or across open ground, and list every other new load planned this year.

Ask the contractor whether the charger and the other planned equipment were assessed together or one at a time, and whether a longer route changes the equipment or protection proposed.

What should the assessment establish?

Which path brings supply out to the parking position when the charger stands some distance from the house at a Woodstock address? The route is traced, and any detached garage it passes through is shown. Is the charger weighed by itself? No. It is counted with the other loads planned for that year, and a load-management arrangement, if one is proposed, is set out plainly in the scope. What lies outside the quote, and what does the notification cover? A written reply answers both. Outdoor-rated equipment is named in the scope along with its mounting position.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can the charger go on the garage if it is detached from the house?

Possibly, but the feeder to the garage then has to carry the charger as well as everything already there. Ask the contractor to assess that route and say whether the existing supply to the garage can be reused.

Should I wait to install a charger until other upgrades are done?

Not necessarily, though sequence matters. A later panel change can affect a charger installed first, so ask how each planned upgrade would alter that charger's circuit and route.
